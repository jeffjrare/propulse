## Law 25 (Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, CQLR c. P-39.1)

"Law 25" is the amending act (S.Q. 2021, c. 25; its English title was not read). It amended P-39.1, which is the act to
cite. In force in phases: September 2022, September 2023 (impact assessments, s. 17, consent, privacy by default), and
September 22, 2024 (data portability). [CAI, P-39.1, 2026-10-31]

- **A compliance line at every step** of the method and in every analysis, not a section added at the end.
- **A privacy impact assessment (EFVP)** for any project to acquire, develop or overhaul an information system or
  electronic service delivery system involving personal information. The person in charge of protecting personal
  information is consulted from the outset (s. 3.3). [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]
- **Communication outside Québec** (s. 17) needs an impact assessment first. That assessment weighs the sensitivity of the
  information, the purposes, the protection measures and the legal framework of the destination. The communication is
  allowed only if the assessment shows adequate protection, and it is subject to a written agreement. The same applies when
  someone outside Québec collects, uses or keeps information on the enterprise's behalf, which covers hosting. For the CAI,
  "outside Québec" means another province as well as another country. Every chosen service has its region named.
  [P-39.1; CAI, "Utilisation et communication de renseignements personnels", 2026-10-31]
- **Service providers** receiving personal information (s. 18.3) work under a written mandate or contract. It states the
  measures that protect confidentiality, limits use to the mandate, and requires destruction at its end. The provider
  reports any breach to the person in charge without delay and allows verifications. [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]
- **The person in charge of protecting personal information** is the person with the highest authority (s. 3.1). That
  person may delegate all or part of the function in writing. The title and contact details are published on the website,
  or made available by other appropriate means. [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]
- **Consent** (s. 14) is clear, free, informed and given for specific purposes. It is requested for each purpose, in clear
  and simple language, and presented separately when written. [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]
- **Identification, location and profiling** (s. 8.1): before using such a technology, the enterprise informs the person of
  its use and of the means to activate those functions. The CAI reads this as functions off by default (Guidelines 2023-1,
  § B.4). [P-39.1; CAI, "Critères de validité du consentement", 2026-10-31]
- **Privacy by default** (s. 9.1): a technological product or service offered to the public provides the highest level of
  confidentiality by default, with no action by the person. [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]
- **Retention** (s. 23): once the purposes are achieved, the information is destroyed or anonymised, subject to any
  retention period set by another act. P-39.1 sets no period per category. The enterprise's governance policies (s. 3.2)
  set them, so each category of data needs its period. Information used to make a decision about a person is kept at least
  one year after the decision (s. 11). [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]

## Employees

- **Geolocation** (vehicles, phones): tracking a vehicle is tracking its driver. The CAI applies a necessity test: a real,
  important and legitimate objective; a rational link between the collection and the objective; no less intrusive
  alternative; a benefit greater than the intrusion. The employer must show proportionality, and the employee's consent
  does not lift that duty. In practice: a written purpose, employees informed, no tracking outside working hours, limited
  access, short retention. [CAI, "Pointage électronique des employés par géolocalisation : des règles à respecter",
  2021-06-08; the page is gone from cai.gouv.qc.ca and was read in an archived copy, 2026-10-31]
- **No one may be required to stay connected to a device that reveals where they are**, unless an act expressly provides
  for it for health or public-security reasons (Act to establish a legal framework for information technology, CQLR
  c. C-1.1, s. 43). [C-1.1, 2026-10-31]
- **An employee's voice is personal information** (voice notes, recordings).

## Calls and messages

- **Recording a call:** the Criminal Code (s. 184) makes intercepting a private communication an offence. It allows
  interception with the express or implied consent of the person who made it or of the person meant to receive it
  (s. 184(2)(a)). [Justice Canada, 2026-10-31]
- **P-39.1 never mentions recording, but a recording is a means of collection.** The person is therefore informed of it
  and of its purposes (s. 8), and s. 8.1 also applies if the technology can identify the person. Recording employees fares
  poorly under the necessity test. Default: do not record staff's or executives' mobile lines. [P-39.1, 2026-10-31]
- **Commercial electronic messages** (email, SMS) fall under Canada's anti-spam legislation, known as CASL (S.C. 2010,
  c. 23). A message with no commercial purpose falls outside it. Two kinds of message are exempt from consent only:
  messages that facilitate, complete or confirm a transaction already agreed (s. 6(6)(b)), and factual notices about an
  ongoing account or relationship (s. 6(6)(d)). Sender identification and an unsubscribe mechanism (s. 6(2)) still apply
  to them. [Justice Canada, 2026-10-31]
- **Telemarketing:** the CRTC's Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules and the National Do Not Call List, made under the
  Telecommunications Act (s. 41 and ss. 41.1 to 41.7). [Justice Canada; lnnte-dncl.gc.ca, 2026-10-31; **the CRTC's own
  rules page was not read**: crtc.gc.ca refused automated reads]

## Contracts and trades

Flag to counsel depending on the engagement, without concluding:
- **Consumer Protection Act (CQLR c. P-40.1)** for contracts with individuals: formalities, estimates, cancellation,
  legal warranty. [P-40.1, 2026-10-31]
- **RBQ contractor licence** (Building Act, CQLR c. B-1.1, s. 46) depending on the work, and coverage by the **Act
  respecting labour relations, vocational training and workforce management in the construction industry** (CQLR c. R-20)
  depending on the work. [B-1.1, R-20, 2026-10-31]
