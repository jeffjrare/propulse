# Compliance in Québec

**As of:** 2026-10-29

**This is not legal advice.** This module says what to flag and what to ask. Every point becomes a question for the
client's counsel, with what was found and its dated source. **Section numbers are given as commonly cited; confirm them at
the source (LégisQuébec, the Commission d'accès à l'information, Justice Canada) before they decide anything.** None was
re-read at its source on the date above.

## Law 25 (Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, CQLR c. P-39.1)

- **A compliance line at every step** of the method and in every analysis, not a section added at the end.
- **A privacy impact assessment (EFVP)** before any project to acquire, develop or overhaul a system that handles personal
  information (s. 3.3), **and before any communication outside Québec** (s. 17), with a written agreement. A service hosted
  in Ontario or the United States is a communication outside Québec. Every chosen service has its region named.
- **Service providers** receiving personal information have a written mandate.
- **The person in charge of protecting personal information** is the person with the highest authority unless delegated in
  writing; their title and contact details are published.
- **Consent** is requested for specific purposes, separately for each; location and profiling functions are off by default.
- **Retention:** each category has its period; at the end, destruction or anonymisation.

## Employees

- **Geolocation** (vehicles, phones): tracking a vehicle is tracking its driver. The regulator applies a necessity and
  proportionality test: a written purpose, employees informed, no tracking outside working hours, limited access, short
  retention.
- **An employee's voice is personal information** (voice notes, recordings).

## Calls and messages

- **Recording a call:** the Criminal Code (s. 184) forbids interception without the consent of at least one party; Law 25
  requires informing people of the recording and its purpose. Recording employees fares poorly under the necessity test.
  Default: do not record staff's or executives' mobile lines.
- **Commercial electronic messages** (email, SMS): Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL); it covers commercial messages
  only, not exchanges about an ongoing case.
- **Telemarketing:** CRTC rules (National Do Not Call List).

## Contracts and trades

Flag to counsel depending on the engagement, without concluding:
- **Consumer Protection Act (LPC)** for contracts with individuals: formalities, estimates, cancellation, legal warranty.
- **RBQ contractor licence** depending on the work; **coverage by the R-20 Act** (labour relations in the construction
  industry) depending on the work.
